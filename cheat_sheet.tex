\section{Game Basics}

The goal of Riichi Mahjong is to earn more points than the 
other players by winning hands. To win a hand you must build 
a valid hand of 14 tiles before any of your opponents, then 
the quality of your hand will determine how many points you win.

\subsection{What is a Valid Hand}

A valid hand is made of 5 shapes, 4 sets of 3 tiles and 1 pair.
Each set of 3 tiles can either be a consecutive sequence of 
tiles in the same suit or a set of 3 identical tiles. E.g. 
\mmj{234s-22s-333p-567m-777z}
A hand must also contain a Yaku to be valid.

\subsection{Making a Hand}

During the game your hand will consist of 13 tiles, and 
each turn you will draw one tile and discard one tile. The 
goal is to reach a point where the 13 tiles in your hand
only need one more tile to be a valid hand. Being one tile 
away from a valid hand is called 
being in Tenpai (or Ready Hand). An example is below, 
with the 14\textsuperscript{th} tile being one of the tiles 
needed to complete the hand.
\mmj{2345s444p505p789m-5s}

\subsection{Calling Tiles}

Drawing and discarding isn't the only way to add tiles 
to your hand. When other players discard tiles there are 
three different calls you can make to add the discarded 
tile to your hand. Once you have made a call your hand is 
considered open (e.g. you cannot call Riichi). The general steps are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A player discards a tile that completes a sequence or 
set that is partially complete in your hand.
\item Make the corresponding call and take the discarded tile, 
placing it and the tiles from your hand that complete the set 
face up next to your hand.
\item Discard a tile from your hand. You cannot discard a
tile that would have completed the shape instead of the tile 
you took, e.g. you cannot discard \mj{3p} if you took the 
\mj{6p} to complete the \mj{45p} set.
\item The player after you in turn order takes their turn. 
(This can cause a player to miss a turn.)
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Chii}

You can call Chii when the player before you 
discards a tile that can complete a sequence in your hand. 
For example, if you had \mj{57p} in your hand, 
and the player before you discards the \mj{6p} you can 
call Chii.

\subsubsection{Pon}

You can call Pon when any other player discards a 
tile that completes a set of 3 tiles. If you have \mj{77m} in 
your hand you could call Pon for the third \mj{7m}.

\subsubsection{Kan}

Kan is the trickiest call as it has some extra rules. Firstly 
Kan calls create groups of 4 tiles, these function as groups of 
3 for anything that requires a group of 3, but only groups of 4 
can count toward things that specify groups of 4. It also means 
your final hand will have more than 14 tiles, because one or more 
of the shapes will be 4 tiles. There are 3 types of Kan call,
\begin{itemize}
\item Daminkan (Open Kan): This is called like Pon, only if 
you have three of a tile in your hand, you call Kan when 
another player discards the fourth.
\item Ankan (Closed Kan): You can call this Kan if you have 
4 of a tile in your hand. Calling Kan in this way does not 
count as opening your hand, and when you place the tiles 
next to your hand place them such as
\mmj{X55Xs}
\item Shouminkan (Added Kan): If you have previously called 
Pon to create a group of three tiles, and draw the fourth 
tile of the group you can call Kan and add it to the group 
of three.
\end{itemize}
All Kan calls also have two extra rules. After you call, 
you draw an extra tile from the Dead Wall before you discard 
a tile. Finally after a Kan is completed another tile 
in the Dead Wall is revealed to be a new Dora Indicator.

\subsection{Winning}

Once your hand is in Tenpai (it only needs one more tile 
to be a valid hand), there are two calls you can make to 
win the hand.

\subsubsection{Ron}

Ron is called when another player discards the tile you need 
to win. In this case the point value of your hand is 
deducted from their point total and added to your total.

\subsubsection{Tsumo}

Tsumo is called when you draw the tile you need to win. 
In this case the point value of the hand is split and 
paid by all the other players into your total.

\subsection{Riichi}

Riichi Mahjong has a special call you can make called 
Riichi. If you hand is closed (you haven't called Chii, Pon 
or Kan) and it is in Tenpai, you can call Riichi when 
you discard your tile for the turn. When you discard it 
turn the tile sideways to indicate the moment when you 
called Riichi. After calling Riichi, you cannot change the 
contents of your hand, on your turn you only draw a tile 
and discard it until you can call Ron or Tsumo.

\subsection{Furiten}

Furiten is a state your hand can be in, and while your 
hand is in Furiten you cannot win by calling Ron, only 
by calling Tsumo. There are a couple ways to be in Furiten:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If you have previously discarded a tile that could 
complete your hand. 
\item If you call Riichi and skip  
a tile that could complete the hand you are in Furiten.
\item If not in Riichi and skip a tile
that could complete the hand, you are temporarily in Furiten 
until your next discard.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Non-Yaku Han Scorers}

These are features of a hand that provide Han, but do not 
count as Yaku and themselves cannot make a hand valid.

\subsection{Dora: 1 Han}

At the start of the game one of the tiles on the Dead Wall 
is revealed as a Dora Indicator. The dora indicators look 
like,
\mmj{4XXXXp}
Whichever tile comes after the Dora Indicator is a Dora tile, 
so in the above example \mj{5p} would be a Dora tile.
The sequence loops so if \mj{9m} is the Dora Indicator 
then \mj{1m} is a Dora tile.

The Honor tiles also have an order, the Wind tiles are in 
the order of play \mj{1234z}. Similarly the Dragon tiles 
have an order \mj{567z}, but you can remember the sequence 
alphabetically, green to red to white.

\subsection{Kandora: 1 Han}

After a player calls Kan, the next tile of the Dead Wall 
is flipped and becomes another Dora indicator. Continuing 
from the example above the wall might now show 
\mmj{4p7sXXX} 
Which means that \mj{5p} and \mj{8s} are both Dora tiles.

\subsection{Akadora: 1 Han}

In Riichi Mahjong there are often a set of red 5s. Some sets 
have one in each suit, and some have one in each except two in 
the circle suit. These tiles look like \mj{0m0s0p}.

\subsection{Uradora: 1 Han}

If you win a hand after calling Riichi the tiles under the 
the currently revealed Dora Indicators are revealed as 
Uradora Indicators. E.g. if the dora indicators looked like 
\mmj{2z8pXXX}
and you won by Riichi, it might reveal
\mmj{4m7sXXX}
as the Uradora Indicators. Meaning that \mj{3z}, \mj{9p}, 
\mj{5m} and \mj{8s} all give 1 Han.

\subsection{Stacking Dora}

One tile can be a Dora tile multiple times, and thus be 
worth multiple Han. For example, if the Dora Indicators showed 
\mmj{22XXXs}
then \mj{3s} would be worth 2 Han. Similarly if the 
Dora Indicator was
\mmj{4XXXXm}
then \mj{0m} would be worth 2 Han.

\section{Scoring}

\subsection{Calculation}

Once you have won a hand it's time to calculate your score. First 
determine the Han ($h$) and Fu ($f$) count of the hand, then use the 
following formula to calculate the base points of the hand:
$$f\times2^{2+h}.$$
If base points is 2000 or above the hand becomes a Mangan.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Non dealer wins by Tsumo, each player pays base points 
and the dealer pays  $2\times$ base points.
\item Non dealer wins by Ron, the player who dealt in pays 
$4\times$ base points.
\item Dealer wins by Tsumo, each player pays $2\times$ base 
points.
\item Dealer wins by Ron, the player who dealt in pays $6\times$
base points.
\end{enumerate}
The amount a player needs to pay gets rounded up to the nearest 100.

\subsubsection{Mangan and Above}

If you have a hand with 3 Han and 70+ Fu, 4 Han and 40+ Fu or 5 Han, you 
have a Mangan, which is worth 2000 base points. If you have more Han the 
hand can improve according to the table below.

\begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|}
\hline
Han & Name & Base Points \\
\hline
6-7 & Haneman & 3000 \\
8-10 & Baiman & 4000 \\
11-12 & Sanbaiman & 6000 \\
13+ & Kazoe Yakuman & 8000 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Fu}

Hands start with 20 Fu by default, and then after additional 
Fu is counted the score is rounded up to the nearest 10. 

\subsubsection{Fu for Tile Groups}

The tile groups in a hand can give Fu, sequences always 
give 0 Fu.

\begin{tabular}{|lcc|}
\hline
Group Type & Simples & Terminals \& Honors \\
\hline
Triplet (open) & 2 & 4 \\
Triplet (closed) & 4 & 8 \\
Kan (open) & 8 & 16 \\
Kan (closed) & 16 & 32 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

You can think of this as a triplet gives 4 and a Kan gives 16. 
Then halve if it's open and double if it made from honor or 
terminal tiles.

\subsubsection{Fu for Tile Wait}

You can also score a small amount of Fu based on the 
hand's tile wait.

\begin{tabular}{|l|c|}
\hline
Wait Type & Fu \\
\hline
Open Wait (Ryanmen) & 0 \\
Dual Pair Wait (Shanpon) & 0 \\
Closed Wait (Kanchan) & 2 \\
Edge Wait (Penchan) & 2 \\
Pair Wait (Tanki) & 2 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\section{Draw Conditions}

\subsection{Four Wind Discard}

If the first four discards of the game are all the same wind 
tile. 
\mmj{2z-2z-2z-2z}

\subsection{Four Kan Draw}

The game becomes a draw if two or more players have called for
a total of four Kans.

\subsection{Nine Terminals and Honors}

On your first turn if your hand and drawn tile contain 
nine or more terminal or honor tiles you can call for a draw.
(You might also want to pursue 13 Orphans.)

\subsection{Four Riichi}

The game becomes a draw if all four players have called Riichi.

\subsection{Triple Ron}

If three players call Ron on the same tile, the game is a draw.

\section{Three Player Mode}

The three player mode is mostly the same, but with some rule 
modifications.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Chii is no longer a call you can make.
\item The \mj{2345678m} tiles are removed, although you can 
choose a different suit. If the \mj{1m} tile is the Dora 
Indicator then \mj{9m} is the Dora.
\item The Dead Wall has 8 tiles to use as Kan/Kita draws instead 
of 4. This means that the first Dora Indicator is the 5\textsuperscript{th} 
tile rather than the 3\textsuperscript{rd} tile on the wall.
\item There is no longer a north seat. This means that the 
seats are always east, south, and west when moving anti-clockwise
from the dealer. This may mean that the seats no longer represent
cardinal directions.
\item North tiles are treated differently. They can 
serve as Yakuhai for all players and/or be treated as Nukidora. 
With the Nukidora rule, players with a \mj{4z} in their
hand can call Kita placing the tile in front of them and the 
tile then scores 1 han. After a Kita call you draw a new 
tile from the Dead Wall (this can count for the Rinshan Kaihou
yaku).
\end{enumerate}
